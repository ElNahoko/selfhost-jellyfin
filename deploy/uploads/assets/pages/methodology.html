Who we are and how it works

Nahoko is a free guide to films, series, anime and games: what is worth watching or playing, who made it, and what people think of it.

Who is behind Nahoko

One developer, as a passion project. Nahoko runs on a small personal server. There is no company and no investor behind it, and it has no link with IMDb, TMDB, TVmaze, Wikipedia, YouTube or any streaming service.

Where the information comes from

  • IMDb (its free daily datasets): titles, years, genres, running times, ratings and vote counts, cast and crew, and the list of episodes of every series. Information courtesy of IMDb (imdb.com). Used with permission.
  • TMDB: posters, plot summaries and portraits. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.
  • TVmaze: wide pictures for series, air dates of episodes, and some cast portraits.
  • AniList: which series and films are anime.
  • Wikidata and Wikipedia: games, the countries of films, and facts about people (birth, death, short biography). Wikipedia texts are under the CC BY-SA licence and each one links to its article.
  • Platforms: which service released a film or a series (Netflix, Apple TV+, Disney+…), from Wikidata and TVmaze. It tells where a title comes from; Nahoko links to no service and sells nothing.
  • YouTube: trailers, and a still from the trailer as the wide picture on film pages.
  • News: headlines and short summaries from the publishers’ own public feeds, always credited to them. We never copy whole articles.

How it is kept up to date

The catalogue is rebuilt every week from IMDb’s datasets, quietly in the background: the old one stays online until the new one is ready. Posters and pictures are fetched the first time someone needs them, then kept. News is checked every 30 minutes. Facts about people are refreshed every month.

Which titles are in the catalogue

Titles that many people have seen and rated: films with tens of thousands of votes on IMDb, series with a little fewer. Then, for each country, its best-known national films and series, so that great titles from Egypt, Korea, Italy or India are not drowned out by Hollywood. Plus the 5,000 best-known anime and thousands of games for PC, PlayStation, Xbox and Switch. No one can pay to be listed or to be shown first.

What the ratings mean

  • Films and series: the IMDb user rating, out of 10, with the number of votes.
  • “Top rated” shelves: sorted by a score that also weighs the number of votes, so a 9.1 from 2,000 votes does not beat 12 Angry Men.
  • Episodes: each episode’s IMDb rating, shown as a coloured chart per season (or per block of 25 episodes for very long seasons).
  • Games: the critics’ score (Metacritic) when Wikidata has it.

People pages

Every actor, director and writer of a catalogue title has a page: their portrait, date and place of birth, a short biography from Wikipedia, their best-known titles and everything they made that is in the catalogue. The filmography is limited to the catalogue, so it is not always complete.

Automatic texts

Some short texts are written automatically from our own data, for example the “Background” notes under a news story (year, rating, cast of the titles it mentions). They are facts from the catalogue, not opinions. Plot summaries come from TMDB.

Independence

No ads, no affiliate links, no tracking, no paid placement. Nahoko does not host or sell any film. Signing in is optional and only keeps your favorites; see the privacy page.

Limits

  • Our sources can be wrong or incomplete, and we can only be as right as they are.
  • Some titles or people may have no picture or no description yet.
  • Only titles in the catalogue have their own page.

How to report an error

Write to us on the contact page: say which page and what is wrong. Errors that come from IMDb, TMDB or Wikipedia are best corrected there too, so everyone benefits.
